# Why the controller was built this way

The owner accepted the practical cooling/noise result and ended further qualification. This decision history accompanies the measured results; an omitted proof is not an achieved guarantee. The final source, configuration, evidence manifest and release assets must be read together. Formal performance noninferiority remained unresolved, and the two-hour soak was explicitly waived.

## The problem and the owner's operating objective

The owner wanted the four cards to behave as one cooling unit. All eight fans therefore receive one common integer target selected from the hottest valid core reading, regardless of which card occupies the top slot. The operating preference is roughly 75–80 C under sustained work, with normal peaks up to 82 C accepted. A 67 C result obtained with loud 100% fans would not meet that preference better than a quiet 78 C result.

The original automatic-control baseline reached 90 C after 259.80 seconds under the recorded native workload, with the hottest card's fan command around 54%. That run, rather than an assumption about stacked cards, demonstrated the need for custom management on this installation. It also established why automatic control can bridge a short recovery without being an indefinite substitute for the custom curve during that load.

The earlier two-card experiments showed that a neighboring card's fans can improve cooling even when that card is idle. They also showed that colder operation did not invariably improve request latency at a fixed board-power budget. Those results motivate coordinated fans and direct performance measurement. They do not provide a measured four-card spacing coefficient. See [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md) for the earlier validated populations and limitations.

## Decisions, observed problems, and the resulting proof

| Decision | Why it exists | Evidence and scope |
|---|---|---|
| Keep every requested cap at 275 W | The study and native four-card recipe share this configured power budget. A fan test must not quietly gain performance by raising it. | Every comparison retains requested and enforced ranges for all four UUIDs. Reported instantaneous power can exceed the setting; 275 W is not claimed as a calibrated electrical ceiling. |
| Control by exact UUIDs and the hottest valid core | GPU indices can change and the controlling card need not always be the physical top card. | Identity, duplicates, finite values, fan counts, sample age and boot ID are checked. Firmware-backed slot order is descriptive evidence, not an input to fan demand. |
| Use a curve rather than a flat 80% target | A flat setting gives neither quiet idle nor room for warmer operation. The nominal fixed80 screen briefly required 83% and was not qualified as flat80. | The JSON table, active floor, startup floor, cooling filter and protective demand are recorded separately. Requested and reported means, peaks and change rates show their actual effect. |
| Increase protection gradually | Jumping directly to 100% at 82 C would impose a large noise change before the owner's accepted operating limit required it. | Raw-temperature protection uses 81/83%, 82/86%, 84/92%, 85/95%, 88/100%. The selected JSON and policy source are the authority. |
| Smooth cooling and avoid rewriting unchanged targets | Small temperature oscillations should not cause constant speed commands. Cooling can slow gradually while heating gets a prompt response. | Three-second cooling filter, three-point deadband, twenty-second dwell and one-point reductions at ten-second intervals. Full command logs retain transitions and changes per minute. |
| Confirm all eight policies and readbacks | A successful setter call alone cannot establish that every fan entered the intended mode. | The adapter checks exact targets/policies, positive reported RPM and bounded speed settling. CPU cases cover partial writes, failed restoration and unsettled fans. Driver observations are not independent tachometry. |
| Use a permanent kernel writer lease | Restarting or removing a lock pathname can permit two processes to believe they own fan control. A stopped process still exists. | Actual STOP retains the lease; KILL releases the same inode. A competing installed writer was rejected before hardware access. Updates and removal preserve that inode. |
| Add a separate observer and systemd watchdogs | A controller cannot supervise itself while stopped, killed or blocked. | Independent GPU sampling, fresh current-boot state, six-second watchdogs and restart supervision. Current-source idle STOP/KILL proofs exist; current-source hot proofs and real reboot are separate acceptance items. |
| Restore verified automatic control before suspending ordinary work | The owner preferred continuity when visibility is briefly lost. Returning to automatic is a measurable handoff rather than simply abandoning manual control. | Restoration attempts all eight fans and verifies automatic policy. The current idle proofs observed automatic handoff in 0.54–5.82 seconds. Custom readiness followed in 3.52–14.87 seconds. These idle timings are not hot-load guarantees. |
| Keep a nonrenewable recovery deadline | Repeated service restarts must not grant an unlimited period of unprotected inference. | A root-owned current-boot recovery episode persists across retries. An actual invalid configuration restored automatic control in 0.48 seconds and confirmed suspension 31.13 seconds after the epoch. Thirty seconds is the request threshold, not zero-latency confirmation. |
| Validate and control only the owned workload | A Docker communication failure should not defeat suspension or affect unrelated containers. | Exact container identity, process start time and cgroup validation. An actual kernel freeze with Docker RPC deliberately unavailable completed in 0.030 seconds and was explicitly unfrozen after fresh protection. The Docker daemon itself was not restarted for that proof. |
| Gate model startup and explicit resumption | Service ordering alone does not establish current hardware readiness after a boot or repair. | The gate requires both fresh guards and no critical latch. A protection-ready status is distinct from an authenticated model-ready API. Real cold boot and inference still require their own receipts. |
| Use deterministic software for control | Fan decisions must not depend on an LLM, its availability, its latency or its output quality. | The controller uses a validated table and finite state transitions. DSV is the measured workload; it does not design or execute real-time cooling decisions. Codex retained the engineering and acceptance boundary. |

## Measurement defects were retained and repaired

Eight fan reads and writes happen sequentially. An early lower1 screen captured two apparently mixed snapshots during a common-target transition. Reviewing the command and read timestamps found that the uniform writes completed about 10 and 18 milliseconds after those reads. The original run kept its failed label; it was not relabeled to make the candidate qualify.

The repaired benchmark brackets each readback with fresh primary observations. Accepted observations require one unchanged common declaration and eight matching manual targets. Rejected raw reads are retained separately, including their temperatures and caps. A bounded, nonrenewable 250-millisecond retry begins only after the first incoherent read. The initial implementation wrongly applied that allowance to an already coherent 306-millisecond initial query and failed a trial after eight requests. That procedural failure was preserved; the corrected sampler retains the normal five-second sensor bound for its first query. Six CPU cases exercise the revised measurement contract.

The original twenty-minute comparison's third reference failed its closing slope check; the one prospectively allowed repeat also failed. A short closing regression can depend on where prefill/decoding cycles fall in the window, but inspection also found a real cooling trend in one run. Neither result was promoted retrospectively. A new prospective thirty-minute protocol instead evaluates the last six fully observed workload-cycle means, with coverage and span requirements and the unchanged 0.3 C/min slope limit. Raw peaks, caps, missing observations and thermal events remain independent constraints. Genuine trends and rejected-read peaks are covered by eight CPU cases.

The first new lower2 pair then failed both the cycle plateau and the predeclared performance gate's attainable bound. It held a raw 81 C peak and had only about 0.89% lower warmed decode, but whole-run throughput was about 4.00% lower than its reference. That failure illustrates why neither temperature nor decode alone replaces the complete declared comparison. The replacement series uses an entirely new set of pairs; no earlier pair is pooled into its confidence interval. See [DECODE.md](DECODE.md) for delivery-window definitions and the limited early-stop argument.

The base80 series subsequently completed two thermally eligible pairs, but its third reference cooled by about 0.38 C/min on both middle cards and failed the prospective complete-cycle criterion as well as the original raw criterion. Its peak was only 76 C. An absolute steadiness rule rejects meaningful cooling too; that is a comparability failure rather than evidence of unsafe cooling. The wrapper stopped before a third candidate, and the three-pair performance claim remains unproven. Keep this failed reference visible even if a later, separately declared replacement supplies an eligible pair. Changing the limit after seeing this result would not constitute passing the original gate.

## Acoustic observations are not a PSU diagnosis

The owner heard occasional clicks during inference and paused testing to investigate. An unloaded GPU fan ramp to 80% did not reproduce the clicking. A replacement of the same HELA 2500Rz model also clicked; toggling its zero-RPM switch did not remove the reported behavior. The owner reported no clicking while an unplugged original PSU fan was spun and allowed to coast, and later localized the sound to a small mechanical relay.

These are useful recorded observations, not an instrumented electrical diagnosis or a proven causal link to fan-control polling. They do not establish that either PSU was defective or that the sound was harmless. Testing resumed at the owner's instruction. Acoustic noise in the comparative study is represented by requested/reported GPU fan percentage, not click counts or measured sound pressure.

## What durability can and cannot mean

The package aims for recoverable software behavior: independent supervision, finite native-call bounds, verified automatic restoration, owned-workload suspension, boot gating, versioned backups and bounded logs. It cannot promise that a controller will never stop, that every physical fan will work, or that every kernel/driver/power failure will be recoverable. A real fault receipt proves only the installed version and path exercised at the recorded temperature and workload.

The final package retains the exact selected profile, actual byte-identical installation/automatic-restoration evidence, bounded-log cold native startup and routing proof. Current-source hot faults, a real host reboot and the two-hour soak were not performed after the owner ended additional qualification; the soak was explicitly waived. The interrupted C1 trace is retained without calling it a hardware failure. [FAULT-EVIDENCE.md](FAULT-EVIDENCE.md) distinguishes achieved and omitted evidence; [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md) covers restoration and rollback.
